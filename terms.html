These are the rules for LearnGeo, the geography study site at www.learngeo.app. They cover accounts, classes, and the live screen share. Read the screen section if a child uses LearnGeo. A child’s click is not permission to watch them.

For the person who runs LearnGeo

This page is a plain-language draft of the rules, written to match how the site works today. It is not a lawyer’s opinion, and it is not a parent’s signature. The app does not ask a child’s age and does not store a parent’s signed consent. Do not watch a child under 13 until a parent or a school has agreed in a way you can show later.

LearnGeo does not save a video of a share. If you have been saving pictures or videos of students’ screens on your own computer, these terms do not make that lawful. Stop, tell the parent or the school, and delete those files unless you already have permission that specifically allows a saved recording.

A finished children’s privacy notice also needs the operator’s postal address and phone number. Those are not published yet. Add them here, then have a lawyer read the page, before you treat it as the notice you give families.

Who these terms bind

LearnGeo is operated by the person who controls www.learngeo.app and the site-owner account listed under Contact. There is no separate company name on this page yet. “We” and “the operator” mean that person. “You” means the person using the site. If you are a parent, guardian, or school agreeing for a child, “you” also means you, and the duties below are yours to carry out for that child.

An adult accepts these terms by creating an account, joining or running a class, or clicking Share screen after the on-screen explanation. A child under 13 does not accept them by clicking. The parent or the school does, and only after the rules in Children and Schools are met. If you do not agree, do not create an account and do not share a screen. You can still use the guest study modes, which stay in the browser.

The service

LearnGeo helps people learn countries, capitals, flags, and where places are on a map. It includes Learn, practice tests, class quizzes, games, a student home, and Geo Classroom for a teacher’s class, assignments, and gradebook. Some of the map art comes from other people and stays under their licenses. Map tiles can come from OpenStreetMap or from a map provider a person pastes a key for in settings.

You can study without an account. Progress then stays in that browser’s local storage. Clearing the browser’s site data clears it. We do not get a copy.

Diamonds, XP, cosmetics, and similar rewards are part of the game. They are not money, and they have no cash value. A subscription preview inside an admin tool, if you can see one, is a test screen. It does not charge anyone.

Accounts

An account uses an email and a password, through our account host. On a device that supports it, you can also add a passkey and sign in with the device’s face, fingerprint, or screen lock. When you create an account, the save already on that browser is copied up, including mastery, XP, diamonds, purchases, and settings, so the same save can open on another device.

A teacher who approves a student seat can set that student’s password, and can reset it later, for seats the class system created. The teacher should give that password only to the student or the parent, and should not reuse a password from another service. Email addresses are not always verified. A class code plus the teacher’s approval is the gate for a class seat, which means a wrong email can still be a real account until the teacher rejects it.

You are responsible for the activity on your account. Tell us if you think someone else is using it. We may refuse, suspend, or close an account that breaks these terms, that we are asked to close by a parent or school, or that we cannot secure. A banned account cannot start a screen share.

Children

LearnGeo is used by students, including children under 13. The United States Children’s Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) treats information collected from those children as something an operator generally may collect only with verifiable consent from a parent, or with consent from a school when the school is using the service for the school’s own educational purpose and for no other commercial purpose. Other places have their own rules, including the United Kingdom and the European Economic Area. Those rules still apply when they are stricter.

Personal information here includes a child’s name, email, class, scores, the LearnGeo page they are on, and any live picture of a window they share. A live picture can accidentally include more than LearnGeo: another tab, a password, email, or a room at home. That is why the share step tells the person to pick a browser tab, and why a whole screen is a bad choice.

What a child’s click does not do

Share screen, Not now, and Create account are buttons in the product. For a child under 13, pressing them does not give us parental consent. It does not let us watch that child. It does not let a teacher require the share. A parent’s consent has to come from the parent, or from a school that has the authority to agree for the parent and that has told the family. We have to be able to show that agreement later. The product does not collect it automatically.

If we learn the agreement is missing

If we learn that a child under 13 has an account, or has shared a screen, without a parent or a school agreement, we will stop watching, close the share, and delete the account when a parent or school asks us to, or when we can see that nobody with authority agreed. Students 13 and older may still need a parent under the law of their state or country. When that is so, the same rule applies: the adult agrees, not the click.

Do not create an account for yourself if you are under 13. Ask a parent or your school.

Live screen sharing

This is the part that matters most for families. Read it even if you only use the class tools.

What actually happens

A signed-in person on Chrome or Edge on a computer can click Share screen. The button is not offered as a working share on a phone. Before anything is sent, the site explains the share, and the browser then opens its own window picker. Nothing is captured before that person picks a window or tab and confirms it in the browser. The browser shows its own sharing indicator. Stop sharing ends the share. Signing out ends it too. A guest cannot share, and a guest is not put on the watch list.

The picture is live. It is a video stream of the window or tab that person picked, for as long as they leave sharing on. The camera and the microphone are off. LearnGeo does not ask for them for this feature. The stream goes through Daily.co, in a private room named for that account, so the picture can be shown to the one person allowed to watch. LearnGeo’s database does not receive the pixels. It stores only whether the share is live, the room name, when it started, and the last time the device checked in. That is one row per account, overwritten. There is no album of past screens.

LearnGeo does not save a video of the share. The meeting is set up so the student and the viewer cannot turn on Daily’s cloud recording from LearnGeo. When Stop sharing runs cleanly, we ask Daily to delete the room. If the browser closes without a clean stop, the share drops off the owner’s list after about two minutes without a check-in, and the meeting token expires within two hours. That leftover time is a live room winding down, not a recording we keep.

Who can watch

Only the site owner, signed in as the owner account and holding an open UltraAdmin session, can watch a live share. A classroom teacher cannot open that picture. Other admins cannot open it. Other students cannot open it. The owner sees one tile at a time, and only for a share that is still live.

The owner may watch only for one of these reasons: a parent or school asked, the school is using the share for a lesson and has already told families, a safety problem, abuse of the service, or keeping the share feature working (for example, confirming that a share started or stopped). Curiosity is not a reason. The owner stops watching when that reason is over. The product’s own note to the owner is the same: stop watching when you are done.

No private recording of a child

The software does not save the picture. The person watching must not save it either. That includes a screenshot, a photo of the monitor, and a recorder running on the owner’s computer. A saved copy of a child’s screen is a different and more serious thing than a live look.

A saved copy is allowed only when all of these are true: a parent or the school has agreed in writing to that saved recording, the agreement names the reason, the reason is a school or safety need, the copy is not used for advertising or for anything outside that reason, and the copy is deleted when the reason is over. Agreeing to use LearnGeo, or clicking Share screen, is not agreement to a saved recording. If a saved copy already exists without that permission, the operator deletes it and tells the parent or the school.

What the student should share

Share a LearnGeo browser tab, not the whole screen, if the browser lets you choose. A whole screen can show other tabs, email, messages, or a camera view of the room. Do not share a window that contains passwords, payment details, or another person’s private information. You can press Not now. You can press Stop sharing at any time. Refusal does not by itself delete your progress or your class seat.

What a teacher must not do

Do not tell a student their teacher can see the live picture. The teacher cannot. Do not make a grade, a prize, or a punishment depend on Share screen. Do not ask a student to share a whole screen. Do not ask a child to share until the school or the parent has been told, in the way the Schools and Parents sections describe. If a student stops sharing, leave it stopped.

Which page you are on

For a signed-in account, LearnGeo also reports the name of the view inside the app: Home, Learn, Practice test, Class quiz, Games, Classroom, and the other named screens. It sends that name and the time. It does not send the answer typed, the country on screen, the score, or a picture. The server keeps one row per account and replaces it. It does not keep a diary of the day. Guests are not reported. A banned account is not reported.

The site owner can see that current page name on the Screen monitoring list. That list is not the live picture. The live picture, when there is one, is a separate block and follows the rules in the previous section. Teachers see class scores and assignments. They do not get this monitoring list from the product.

Classes and teachers

A teacher has a class and a class code. Students who join have a name on the roster. Assignments the teacher saves show up for those students. Scores they hand in show up in the gradebook. The teacher can export the gradebook as a CSV. Announcements go from the teacher to the class. Students do not post back to that stream.

The teacher sees the students’ display names, membership, assignments, and scores. The teacher does not see the live screen and does not see the page-name monitor. The teacher can set or reset the password on a student seat the system created. Treat that as a key to the student’s account: set it in front of the right person, and do not leave it in a shared document.

If you are the teacher and you open student seats, you agree to these terms for that classroom use. You confirm that you have authority from the school or from the parent before a child under 13 is enrolled, and that families have been told about live screen sharing before anyone is asked to turn it on.

Parents

A parent or legal guardian of a child who uses LearnGeo may email the contact below and ask:

  • whether the child has an account, and the name, email, class, and scores on it
  • whether a live share is on right now (we cannot hand over a video, because we do not keep one)
  • that we stop watching and that we not watch that child again
  • that we delete the account
  • what a school told us it was allowed to agree to

We will answer a request from a parent we can reasonably identify. Deleting the account removes the screen-share row with it, because that row belongs to the account. Until the product has a separate switch to disable sharing for one child, deletion and a promise not to watch are how a parent turns the feature off for good. Progress that exists only in a browser’s local storage is on that device. We cannot delete a guest save on a computer we do not hold.

We may ask the school to confirm that you are the parent if the account came through a class. We will not require the child to click through a form as a substitute for you.

Schools

A school may use LearnGeo, including accounts and live screen sharing, for its own teaching. If the school wants children under 13 to have accounts or to share a screen, the school must:

  1. Have authority to agree for the parents for this use, or collect the parents’ consent itself.
  2. Give families notice before the first share. The notice has to say, in ordinary words, that a student may share a live picture of a window they pick, that only the site owner can watch it, that LearnGeo does not save a video, that the camera and microphone stay off, that the student can stop, and that a parent can email us to stop it and to delete the account. The notice also has to describe the page-name line.
  3. Use the information only for the school’s educational purpose. Not for advertising. Not for a product the school sells. Not as a general window into a child’s home screen.
  4. Tell students to share a LearnGeo tab, and not to share a whole screen.
  5. Keep Share screen voluntary. A grade must not depend on it.
  6. Tell us promptly if a parent withdraws agreement, and tell the student to stop sharing.

School consent for a classroom account is not permission for the site owner to record, screenshot, or browse that child’s screen for a reason unrelated to the school’s request, safety, or keeping the feature working. If the school cannot give the notice above, it must not ask students to share, and we must not watch them.

The school is responsible for its own duties under student-record laws, including the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA) where that law applies. LearnGeo is a tool the school chooses. We handle the information described in this page so the school can teach. We do not become the school.

Acceptable use

Use LearnGeo for studying, teaching, and the games on the site. Do not:

  • share someone else’s screen, or a window that shows another person’s private information
  • try to watch another student’s share, or to break the limit that only the site owner can view
  • probe, scan, or attack the site, the account host, or the video host, or try to pull out another person’s save
  • upload or display anything unlawful, or anything that sexually exploits a minor
  • pretend to be a teacher, a parent, or the site owner
  • use the gradebook export to harm a student, or leave a class CSV where strangers can read it
  • scrape the site in a way that degrades it for students

We may remove content, end a share, or close an account to enforce these rules or to protect a child. Ending a share deletes the live room. It does not by itself delete the study progress.

How long it stays

A guest save lasts until that browser clears it. An account lasts until you delete it, a parent or school asks us to delete a child’s account, or we close it under these terms. Class scores stay with the class so the gradebook still works, unless the teacher or a parent asks us to delete a student’s records and we can do that without breaking another student’s data. We will delete or detach a child’s personal information when we no longer need it for the class the school asked us to run.

The live picture is not kept. The status row can remain after a share stops, marked stopped, with the last check-in time. It does not contain the picture. The page-name row keeps the latest name only, until the account is deleted.

Backups at our hosts can lag a deletion by the length of their backup cycle. We do not use a backup as a way to keep watching a child.

Security

Accounts are separated by the database’s row rules. A student writes their own screen-share status and cannot mark someone else as sharing. The list of live shares and the right to watch are checked on the server for the site owner’s session, not by a flag in the browser. The video host’s key stays on the server. The browser receives a short-lived meeting token.

None of that is a promise that a breach is impossible. Tell us if you find a hole, especially one that would let a person other than the site owner see a child’s screen. Do not test that by pointing a share at another child.

No ads from a child’s screen

We do not show third-party advertising inside a lesson. We do not build an advertising profile from a screen share, a page name, a score, or a class roster. We do not let an ad network read a share. If that ever changes, this page has to change first, and a child’s share still cannot be used that way without a new agreement from the parent or the school that names advertising. We do not plan that change.

School records

Scores, roster names, and assignments may be education records of the school. The school decides who on its staff may see them. Live screen contents are not stored here, so we cannot later produce them as a record. A page name and a “share is live” flag are operational data for the owner’s monitoring view. Schools should treat a request from a parent about those the same way they treat other requests about classroom tools, and they can email us to fulfill it.

Stopping and deletion

You may stop using LearnGeo at any time. Stop sharing ends a live picture. Sign out ends the session. Deleting an account, or asking us to delete a child’s account, removes the online save, the screen-share row, and the page-name row tied to that account. A teacher’s gradebook may still need the school’s instruction before a score used for a class is erased. Email the contact below and say what you want deleted.

We may suspend the service, a class, or an account if we have to protect students or if we cannot run the site. We will try to give a school notice when a whole class would go away, unless a safety problem means we have to act first.
